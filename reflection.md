## Week 9 – Customer Segmentation Reflection
In this activity, I used the Wholesale Customers dataset to see how customers could be grouped by their spending habits. I checked for the missing values and duplicates, then standardised the data before I did the clustering.
I tested different K values, and the elbow plot started flattening around K=5. However, K=2 and K=3 both had silhouette scores around 0.55. What I found interesting was that K=3 created one cluster with only two customers. At first, this seemed like a problem, but I also wondered if these could actually be important customers because of their unusually high spending.
K-means performed better than hierarchical clustering, with silhouette scores of 0.55 compared to 0.26. The Calinski-Harabasz scores were also higher for K-means, at 140 compared to 111.
For general marketing, I would probably choose K=2 because the groups are easier to work with. One group mainly spends on fresh products, while the other spends much more on groceries, milk and detergents. However, I wouldn't completely ignore the two unusual customers from K=3. I think they could be worth looking at separately, rather than treating them as just outliers.
